LESSON 01

Modelling Rigid Bodies

In mechanics, real objects are often replaced by mathematical models. A rigid body is a model of an object whose shape and dimensions are assumed to remain unchanged when forces act on it.

Rigid body:

A body for which the distances between all pairs of points are assumed to remain constant.

Why use the rigid-body model?

Real objects deform slightly. A beam bends, a ladder flexes and a metal rod stretches by a small amount. In many mechanics problems these changes are sufficiently small to ignore.

Shape fixed

The geometry of the body does not change while the forces act.

Forces still act

The body may experience several forces, reactions, tensions and weights.

Rotation matters

Unlike a particle model, a rigid body can have a turning effect about a point.

Equilibrium matters

Both translational and rotational equilibrium must be considered.

EXAM TIP

When a question says that a rod or beam is rigid, do not introduce bending or deformation into your equations. Treat the geometry as fixed.

Common modelling assumptions

  • A light rod has negligible mass, so its weight is ignored.
  • A uniform rod has its centre of mass at its midpoint.
  • A smooth contact produces a reaction perpendicular to the contacting surface.
  • A rough contact can provide a frictional force as well as a normal reaction.
  • A particle has size small enough that its rotation does not need to be considered.

LESSON 02

Forces and Lines of Action

A force is completely described by its magnitude, direction and line of action. For moments, the line of action is particularly important.

Line of action:

The straight line extending through the point of application of a force in the direction of the force.

The moment of a force about a point depends on the perpendicular distance between that point and the force's line of action.

M = Fd

where d is the perpendicular distance from the chosen point to the line of action of the force.

Correct distance

Perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action.

Common error

Distance from the pivot to the point where the force happens to be drawn.

Force through the pivot

If the line of action of a force passes directly through the pivot, its perpendicular distance from the pivot is zero. Therefore its moment is zero.

d = 0  ⇒  M = 0
IMPORTANT

A force can be very large and still produce zero moment about a point if its line of action passes through that point.

LESSON 03

Moments of Forces

The moment of a force about a point measures its turning effect about that point.

Moment of a force:

The product of the force and the perpendicular distance from the point to the force's line of action.

Moment = Force × Perpendicular distance

Therefore:

M = Fd

Units

Force is measured in newtons and distance in metres, so moment is measured in:

N m
N m is not normally written as J for a moment.

A joule and a newton-metre have the same base dimensions, but they represent different physical quantities. A joule is a unit of energy/work; N m in this context represents a turning effect.

Direction of a moment

A force can produce either a clockwise or anticlockwise turning effect about the chosen point.

Clockwise

The force tends to rotate the body clockwise about the chosen point.

Anticlockwise

The force tends to rotate the body anticlockwise about the chosen point.

Sign convention

In algebraic solutions, one direction can be chosen as positive. For example:

  • clockwise = positive
  • anticlockwise = negative

or the reverse. Either convention is valid as long as it is used consistently.

LESSON 04

Equilibrium of a Rigid Body

A rigid body is in static equilibrium when it has no translational acceleration and no rotational acceleration.

This requires two independent conditions.

Translational equilibrium

The resultant force is zero.

ΣF = 0

Rotational equilibrium

The resultant moment is zero.

ΣM = 0

Horizontal and vertical equilibrium

For a two-dimensional problem, it is often useful to resolve forces horizontally and vertically.

ΣFₓ = 0
ΣFᵧ = 0

Together with one moment equation, these provide the equilibrium conditions for a planar rigid body.

Full static equilibrium:

The resultant force must be zero and the resultant moment about any point must also be zero.

Do not use moments alone.

A body can have zero resultant moment about one point while still having a non-zero resultant force. Such a system is not in complete static equilibrium.

LESSON 05

Taking Moments About a Point

Taking moments about a carefully selected point is one of the most powerful techniques in rigid-body statics.

Principle of moments:

For a body in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about a point equals the sum of the anticlockwise moments about that point.

Σ clockwise moments = Σ anticlockwise moments

Why choose a particular pivot?

Suppose a beam is supported at A and B and the reaction at A is unknown. If moments are taken about A, the reaction at A has zero moment because its line of action passes through A.

This removes that unknown from the moment equation.

SMART PIVOT STRATEGY

Choose a point through which as many unknown forces as possible act. Their moments then become zero.

Taking moments about different points

If a rigid body is in equilibrium, the resultant moment is zero about any point.

The choice of point changes the appearance of the equation, not the physical equilibrium condition.

LESSON 06

Weight and Centre of Mass

The gravitational force acting on a body is called its weight.

W = mg

where:

  • W is weight in newtons
  • m is mass in kilograms
  • g is acceleration due to gravity

In Edexcel mechanics questions, the value of g should normally be taken from the value specified in the question or from the standard value expected by the paper.

Centre of mass

The weight of a body acts vertically downward through its centre of mass.

For a uniform rod, the centre of mass is at its geometrical midpoint.

Uniform rod → centre of mass at midpoint
UNIFORM ROD EXAMPLE

A uniform rod of length 6 m has weight 80 N. It is pivoted at one end. Find the moment of its weight about the pivot.

The weight acts at the midpoint:

6 ÷ 2 = 3 m

Therefore:

M = 80 × 3

M = 240 N m

COMMON TRAP

Do not automatically place the weight at the end of a uniform rod. The weight acts through the centre of mass, which is at the midpoint.

LESSON 07

Couples

A couple consists of two equal, opposite and parallel forces whose lines of action are separated.

Properties of a couple:
  • The two forces have equal magnitude.
  • The forces act in opposite directions.
  • The forces are parallel.
  • The resultant force is zero.
  • The couple still produces a non-zero turning effect.
Couple moment = Force × Perpendicular separation

If the force magnitude is F and the perpendicular distance between the two lines of action is d:

M = Fd

Why is a couple special?

A single force may produce both translation and rotation. A pure couple produces rotation without a resultant force.

COUPLE EXAMPLE

Two equal and opposite forces of 50 N act with perpendicular separation 0.8 m. Find the moment of the couple.

M = Fd

M = 50 × 0.8

M = 40 N m

KEY PROPERTY

The moment of a couple is independent of the point about which moments are taken.

LESSON 08

Rods, Beams and Rigid Members

Many statics questions involve a rod or beam acted on by several forces. The most important first step is to identify every force acting on the body.

Light rods

A light rod has negligible mass, so its own weight is ignored. Only the external forces shown or described in the question are included.

Uniform rods

A uniform rod has its centre of mass at its midpoint, so its weight acts vertically downward at the midpoint.

General force balance

For a horizontal beam:

Σ vertical forces = 0

For example, if upward reactions are R₁ and R₂ and downward loads are W₁ and W₂:

R₁ + R₂ = W₁ + W₂

A second equation is normally obtained by taking moments about one support.

BEAM WITH TWO SUPPORTS

A light horizontal beam is 8 m long. It carries a 120 N load 4 m from support A. The supports are at A and B. Find the reactions at A and B.

Take moments about A:

RB × 8 = 120 × 4

8RB = 480

RB = 60 N

Now use vertical equilibrium:

RA + 60 = 120

RA = 60 N

Unequal loads

If a load is not centred between two supports, the reactions are generally unequal. The support closer to the larger moment-producing load may carry the larger reaction.

LESSON 09

Support Reactions

Supports exert forces on bodies. The direction and type of reaction depend on the model used in the question.

Pin / hinge

A hinged support can provide horizontal and vertical reaction components but does not allow the body to translate through the support.

Smooth contact

The reaction acts perpendicular to the surface at the point of contact.

String / cable

A string provides tension along the string and pulls rather than pushes.

Support reaction

The reaction direction should be determined from the geometry and contact conditions given.

Two-support strategy

  1. Draw all known forces and unknown reactions.
  2. Use the moment equation to find one reaction.
  3. Use force equilibrium to find the remaining reaction.
  4. Check that the final values satisfy both equations.
EXAM TIP

Taking moments about one support usually removes the reaction at that support from the equation. This is often the fastest route to the other reaction.

LESSON 10

Ladders and Contact Problems

A ladder is modelled as a rigid body. The forces acting on it depend on the contact conditions at its ends.

For a ladder resting against a wall, identify:

  • the weight of the ladder
  • the reaction at the ground
  • the reaction at the wall
  • any frictional forces if the surfaces are rough
  • any applied forces or loads

Smooth surfaces

At a smooth surface there is no frictional force. The reaction is normal to the surface.

For example, at a smooth vertical wall the reaction is horizontal. At a smooth horizontal floor the normal reaction is vertical.

Rough surfaces

A rough surface may provide both a normal reaction and friction. The direction of friction opposes the tendency of relative motion.

FRICTION DIRECTION

Do not automatically assume that friction always acts towards the wall or away from the wall. Determine the direction by considering the tendency of motion.

Ladder method

  1. Draw the ladder as a rigid body.
  2. Mark its weight through its centre of mass.
  3. Draw every contact reaction in the correct direction.
  4. Resolve forces horizontally and vertically if necessary.
  5. Take moments about the most convenient point.
  6. Solve the simultaneous equations.

LESSON 11

Forces at an Angle

Not every force acts perpendicular to the rod. When a force is inclined, the moment can be found using either the perpendicular distance to the line of action or a resolved component.

Using the perpendicular component

If a force F acts at angle θ to the position vector from the pivot to the point of application:

M = Fr sin θ

because the perpendicular component of the force is F sin θ.

M = (F sin θ)r

Using perpendicular distance

Alternatively, determine the perpendicular distance d from the pivot to the line of action:

M = Fd

Both methods give the same physical moment when the geometry is handled correctly.

ANGLED FORCE EXAMPLE

A 40 N force acts at an angle of 30° to a rod. The point of application is 3 m from the pivot. Find the moment.

M = Fr sin θ

M = 40 × 3 × sin 30°

M = 120 × 0.5

M = 60 N m

ANGLE TRAP

Make sure θ is the angle between the force and the line from the pivot to the point of application. If the diagram gives a different angle, convert it before using F r sin θ.

LESSON 12

The Complete Exam Method

A strong statics solution is systematic. Use the following process rather than immediately writing an equation.

  1. Identify the body. Decide exactly which object you are analysing.
  2. Draw all forces. Include weight, reactions, tension, applied forces and friction where appropriate.
  3. Locate the centre of mass. For a uniform rod, this is the midpoint.
  4. Choose a moment point. Look for a point that removes one or more unknown reactions.
  5. Write a moment equation. Use perpendicular distances or resolve angled forces.
  6. Use force equilibrium. Resolve horizontally and vertically where required.
  7. Solve algebraically. Keep exact values until the final stage where practical.
  8. Check signs and units. Re-read the geometry and make sure every moment direction is correct.
HIGH-VALUE EXAM HABIT

Before calculating, ask: “What point should I take moments about?” This single decision often determines how simple the entire solution becomes.

LESSON 13

Common Mistakes and Exam Traps

Mistake Correct approach
Using distance to the force rather than perpendicular distance Use the shortest perpendicular distance to the line of action.
Forgetting a force through the pivot has zero moment Its perpendicular distance is zero.
Putting a uniform rod's weight at the end Place the weight at the midpoint.
Mixing clockwise and anticlockwise signs Choose one sign convention and maintain it.
Using moments without force equilibrium For full static equilibrium, also require ΣF = 0.
Using the wrong angle in Fr sin θ θ must be the angle between the force and the position vector.
Calling a couple a single force A couple consists of two equal, opposite, parallel forces.
Forgetting support reactions Draw a complete force diagram before calculating.
Wrong units Moment is measured in N m.
Rounding too early Keep sufficient accuracy throughout the calculation.

LESSON 14

Worked Examples — Foundation to Exam Level

EXAMPLE 01 • BASIC MOMENT

A 20 N force acts perpendicular to a rod at a distance of 3 m from a pivot. Find its moment.

M = Fd

M = 20 × 3

M = 60 N m

EXAMPLE 02 • FORCE THROUGH PIVOT

A 100 N force acts along a rod through its pivot. Find its moment.

The line of action passes through the pivot.

Therefore d = 0.

M = 100 × 0

M = 0 N m

EXAMPLE 03 • FINDING AN UNKNOWN FORCE

A 30 N force acts 2 m from a pivot. An unknown force F acts 3 m from the pivot on the opposite side. The rod is in equilibrium. Find F.

Clockwise moment = anticlockwise moment.

30 × 2 = F × 3

60 = 3F

F = 20 N

EXAMPLE 04 • UNIFORM ROD

A uniform rod of length 5 m and weight 100 N is hinged at one end. Find the moment of its weight about the hinge.

The weight acts at the midpoint.

Distance = 5/2 = 2.5 m

M = 100 × 2.5

M = 250 N m

EXAMPLE 05 • TWO SUPPORTS

A 10 m beam carries a 200 N load at a point 4 m from the left support A. The right support is B. Find the reaction at B.

Take moments about A.

RB × 10 = 200 × 4

10RB = 800

RB = 80 N

EXAMPLE 06 • FIND THE OTHER REACTION

Using the previous example, find the reaction at A.

Vertical equilibrium:

RA + RB = 200

RA + 80 = 200

RA = 120 N

EXAMPLE 07 • ANGLED FORCE

A 60 N force acts at 40° to a rod. Its point of application is 2 m from the pivot. Find its moment.

M = Fr sin θ

M = 60 × 2 × sin 40°

M ≈ 120 × 0.6428

M ≈ 77.1 N m

EXAMPLE 08 • COUPLE

Two equal and opposite forces of 80 N form a couple whose perpendicular separation is 0.5 m. Find the couple moment.

M = Fd

M = 80 × 0.5

M = 40 N m

EXAMPLE 09 • SEVERAL LOADS

A light horizontal beam has a pivot at its left end. A 20 N force acts downward at 2 m and a 35 N force acts upward at 5 m. Find the resultant moment about the pivot.

Take anticlockwise as positive.

35 × 5 − 20 × 2

= 175 − 40

Resultant moment = 135 N m anticlockwise

EXAMPLE 10 • CHOOSING THE PIVOT

A beam is supported at A and B. Both support reactions are unknown. Explain why taking moments about A is useful.

The reaction at A acts through A.

Therefore its perpendicular distance from A is zero.

Its moment about A is therefore zero.

This leaves an equation involving the reaction at B and the known loads, allowing B to be found directly.

EXAMPLE 11 • FULL EQUILIBRIUM

A horizontal beam is 6 m long. It is supported at both ends. A 120 N downward load acts 2 m from the left support. The beam itself has weight 60 N acting at its midpoint. Find both support reactions.

Let the reactions be RA and RB.

Take moments about A:

RB(6) = 120(2) + 60(3)

6RB = 240 + 180

6RB = 420

RB = 70 N

Vertical equilibrium:

RA + 70 = 120 + 60

RA = 110 N

EXAMPLE 12 • UNKNOWN POSITION

A 50 N load is placed at a distance x from a pivot. A 20 N load acts 4 m from the pivot on the opposite side. The system is balanced. Find x.

50x = 20 × 4

50x = 80

x = 1.6 m

EXAMPLE 13 • THREE FORCES

About a point O, a 25 N force produces a clockwise moment of 100 N m. A second force produces an anticlockwise moment of 60 N m. What additional anticlockwise moment is required for equilibrium?

Total clockwise moment = 100 N m.

Existing anticlockwise moment = 60 N m.

Required additional moment:

100 − 60 = 40

40 N m anticlockwise

EXAMPLE 14 • ANGLE AND RESOLUTION

A 100 N force acts at 60° to a 4 m rod from its pivot. Find its moment.

Perpendicular component = 100 sin 60°.

= 86.60 N approximately.

Moment = 86.60 × 4

≈ 346.4 N m

EXAMPLE 15 • CONCEPTUAL CHECK

Two forces have equal magnitude and act at the same perpendicular distance from a pivot. One is clockwise and one is anticlockwise. What is the resultant moment?

The two moments have equal magnitude and opposite direction.

Therefore they cancel.

Resultant moment = 0

LESSON 15

Statics Formula Atlas

Moment = Force × Perpendicular distance
M = Fd
M = Fr sin θ
Weight = mg
ΣFₓ = 0
ΣFᵧ = 0
ΣM = 0
Σ clockwise moments = Σ anticlockwise moments
Couple moment = F × perpendicular separation
Remember:

For a uniform rod, the weight acts through the midpoint. For a force through the chosen pivot, the moment about that pivot is zero.

CHECKPOINT

Quick Statics Check

1. What is the moment of a 15 N force acting at 3 m perpendicular distance?
Show answer

M = 15 × 3 = 45 N m.

2. Why does a force through the pivot have zero moment?
Show answer

Its line of action passes through the pivot, so the perpendicular distance is zero.

3. What are the two conditions for static equilibrium?
Show answer

The resultant force is zero and the resultant moment is zero.

4. Where does the weight of a uniform rod act?
Show answer

At the midpoint, which is the centre of mass of the uniform rod.

5. What is the moment of a 40 N couple separated by 0.75 m?
Show answer

M = 40 × 0.75 = 30 N m.
