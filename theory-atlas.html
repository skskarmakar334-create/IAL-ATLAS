Exam-focused theory, definitions, principles, explanations, processes, practical theory and extended-response guidance for Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry and Biology.

Mathematics
Pure Mathematics · Mechanics · Statistics
Pure Mathematics 1
P1 · WMA11
Algebraic Manipulation
Core Theory

Be able to simplify expressions involving indices, surds, algebraic fractions and brackets. Correct factorisation and use of algebraic identities are essential before solving equations.

Quadratic Equations
Exam Theory

Quadratic equations can be solved by factorisation, completing the square or the quadratic formula. The discriminant determines the nature of the roots.

  • Δ > 0 gives two distinct real roots.
  • Δ = 0 gives one repeated real root.
  • Δ < 0 gives no real roots.
Sequences and Series
Core Theory

An arithmetic sequence has a constant difference between successive terms. A geometric sequence has a constant ratio between successive terms.

Coordinate Geometry
Method

A straight-line equation can be determined from a point and gradient. Parallel lines have equal gradients while perpendicular lines have gradients whose product is −1.

Trigonometric Equations
Exam Theory

When solving trigonometric equations, first find the principal solution and then use the symmetry and periodicity of the relevant trigonometric function to obtain all required solutions in the given interval.

Always check the requested interval and include every valid solution.
Differentiation
Core Theory

Differentiation gives the gradient of a curve. At a stationary point, the first derivative is zero. The second derivative can be used to classify the stationary point.

  • Second derivative positive → local minimum.
  • Second derivative negative → local maximum.
Integration
Core Theory

Integration reverses differentiation. An arbitrary constant must be included for indefinite integrals. Definite integration can be used to calculate areas under curves.

6-Mark Style: Curve Analysis
Long Response

A complete solution should establish the required equation, differentiate accurately, identify stationary points, classify them and interpret the result in the context of the question.

Show the chain of reasoning. Do not give only the final coordinates or answer.
Pure Mathematics 2
P2 · WMA12
Logarithms
Core Theory

A logarithm is the inverse operation to exponentiation. Logarithm laws allow products, quotients and powers to be rewritten in simpler forms.

Exponential Functions
Core Theory

Exponential models describe quantities that grow or decay at a rate proportional to their current value. The natural exponential function has the special property that its derivative is itself.

Binomial Expansion
Exam Theory

The binomial theorem expands a power of a sum into a sequence of terms involving binomial coefficients.

Chain Rule
Method

The chain rule is used when one function is contained inside another. Differentiate the outer function with respect to the inner function and multiply by the derivative of the inner function.

Product Rule
Method

When differentiating the product of two functions, differentiate one function while keeping the other unchanged, then reverse the roles and add the two results.

Trigonometric Identities
Exam Theory

Identities are true for all permitted values of the variable. They can be used to transform complicated trigonometric expressions into a form that can be solved or differentiated.

Pure Mathematics 3
P3 · WMA13
Composite Functions
Core Theory

A composite function applies one function to the output of another. The order matters, so fg(x) and gf(x) generally produce different results.

Inverse Functions
Core Theory

An inverse function reverses the effect of the original function. The original function must satisfy the necessary one-to-one condition on its domain for an inverse to exist.

Newton-Raphson Method
Numerical Method

Newton-Raphson is an iterative method for finding an approximate root of an equation. A starting value is required and repeated iterations move the estimate towards a root.

State the starting value and show sufficient iterations before giving the required accuracy.
Numerical Iteration
Numerical Method

An equation can be rearranged into an iterative form x = g(x). Starting from an initial estimate, repeated substitution can converge to a solution.

Implicit Differentiation
Method

When y cannot conveniently be expressed explicitly in terms of x, differentiate both sides with respect to x and treat y as a function of x.

Integration by Parts
Method

Integration by parts is useful when the integrand is a product of functions and direct integration is not convenient.

Pure Mathematics 4
P4 · WMA14
Vectors
Core Theory

Vectors have both magnitude and direction. They can represent displacement, velocity and other directed quantities.

Scalar Product
Core Theory

The scalar product produces a scalar. It can be used to find the angle between vectors and determine whether two vectors are perpendicular.

Parametric Equations
Method

Parametric equations express x and y in terms of a third parameter, usually t. They are particularly useful for curves that are difficult to describe directly as y=f(x).

Differential Equations
Long Response

A differential equation relates a function to one or more of its derivatives. Separation of variables allows the variables to be placed on opposite sides before integration.

First-Order Differential Equations
Exam Theory

After integration, use the supplied initial condition to determine the arbitrary constant. The final solution should satisfy both the differential equation and the initial condition.

Mechanics 1
M1 · WME01
Particle Model
Mechanics Theory

A particle is treated as having mass but negligible dimensions. This model allows forces to be considered as acting at a single point.

Newton's Laws
Core Theory

Newton's laws describe how forces affect the motion of objects. The resultant force determines acceleration according to Newton's second law.

Connected Particles
Exam Theory

For connected particles, identify the forces acting on each particle separately. Use a consistent positive direction and apply Newton's second law to each particle.

Friction
Core Theory

Friction acts to oppose relative motion or the tendency for relative motion between surfaces. Limiting friction occurs when motion is about to begin.

Momentum Conservation
Long Response

When the external resultant impulse on a system is negligible, the total momentum of the system remains constant.

Energy Conservation
Core Theory

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can be transferred between kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic and other forms.

Projectile Motion
Long Response

Resolve the initial velocity into horizontal and vertical components. Treat the horizontal motion independently from the vertical motion, where acceleration is due to gravity.

For a full projectile solution: resolve → choose equations → solve simultaneously → check the physical meaning of the answer.
Mechanics 2
M2 · WME02
Circular Motion
Core Theory

An object moving in a circle has acceleration directed towards the centre. The force responsible for this acceleration is the resultant radial force.

Collisions
Long Response

Momentum is conserved in an isolated collision. The coefficient of restitution describes the relative speed of separation compared with relative speed of approach.

Centre of Mass
Core Theory

The centre of mass is the point at which the mass of a system can be considered concentrated for translational motion.

Moments and Equilibrium
Exam Theory

For a body in equilibrium, both the resultant force and resultant moment must be zero.

Statistics 1
S1 · WST01
Sampling
Statistics Theory

A sample is a subset of a population. Sampling methods are used when studying the entire population is impractical.

  • Simple random sampling
  • Systematic sampling
  • Stratified sampling
  • Quota sampling
  • Opportunity sampling
Types of Data
Definitions

Data may be qualitative or quantitative. Quantitative data may be discrete or continuous.

Primary and Secondary Data
Definitions

Primary data are collected directly for the investigation. Secondary data were previously collected for another purpose or by another source.

Measures of Location
Statistics Theory

Mean, median and mode describe the central location of data. The choice of measure depends on the distribution and the presence of extreme values.

Measures of Dispersion
Statistics Theory

Range, interquartile range and standard deviation describe how spread out data are. A larger measure of dispersion indicates greater variability.

Outliers
Exam Theory

An outlier is an observation that is unusually distant from the rest of the data. Outliers can have a substantial effect on the mean and standard deviation.

Histograms
Exam Theory

For grouped continuous data, histogram area represents frequency. Therefore class width must be considered when comparing bars.

Cumulative Frequency
Method

Cumulative frequency is the running total of frequencies. A cumulative frequency graph can be used to estimate quartiles, median and percentages below a particular value.

Box Plots
Statistics Theory

A box plot displays the minimum, lower quartile, median, upper quartile and maximum. It allows distributions to be compared using location and spread.

Probability
Core Theory

Probability measures how likely an event is to occur. Probabilities lie between 0 and 1 inclusive.

Independent Events
Statistics Theory

Two events are independent when the occurrence of one does not change the probability of the other.

Correlation
Exam Theory

Correlation measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two variables. Correlation does not establish causation.

Regression
Exam Theory

A regression line provides an equation for predicting one variable from another. Predictions should preferably be made within the range of the observed data.

Coding
Statistics Theory

Coding transforms every observation using a linear transformation. It can simplify calculations while preserving the underlying statistical information.

6-Mark Statistics Question
Long Response

A strong statistical response should identify the correct method, perform the calculation accurately, interpret the result in context and give a conclusion that answers the question directly.

Never stop at a numerical result when the question asks for an interpretation or conclusion.
Statistics 2
S2 · WST02
Discrete Random Variables
Statistics Theory

A discrete random variable takes a countable set of possible values, each associated with a probability.

Binomial Distribution
Distribution Theory

A binomial model applies when there is a fixed number of independent trials, two possible outcomes and a constant probability of success.

Poisson Distribution
Distribution Theory

The Poisson distribution models the number of occurrences of an event within a fixed interval when events occur with a constant average rate and independently.

Normal Distribution
Distribution Theory

The normal distribution is continuous, symmetric and bell-shaped. Its mean determines the centre and its standard deviation determines the spread.

Hypothesis Testing
Long Response

A hypothesis test begins with a null hypothesis and alternative hypothesis. Evidence from a sample is used to determine whether the null hypothesis should be rejected at the stated significance level.

Physics
Mechanics · Materials · Waves · Electricity · Fields · Particles · Cosmology
Unit 1 · Mechanics & Materials
WPH11
Scalars and Vectors
Definitions

A scalar has magnitude only. A vector has both magnitude and direction.

Newton's Laws of Motion
6-Mark Theory

Newton's laws explain the relationship between resultant force, mass and acceleration. In equilibrium the resultant force is zero.

Momentum Conservation
Long Response

For an isolated system, total momentum before an interaction equals total momentum after the interaction.

Principle of Moments
Long Response

For a body in rotational equilibrium, the sum of clockwise moments about a point equals the sum of anticlockwise moments about that point.

Work, Energy and Power
Core Theory

Work is the transfer of energy by a force acting through a distance. Power is the rate of energy transfer.

Hooke's Law
Materials

For an elastic material below the limit of proportionality, extension is proportional to applied force.

Stress, Strain and Young Modulus
6-Mark Theory

Stress measures force per unit cross-sectional area. Strain measures fractional change in length. Young modulus describes the stiffness of a material in the elastic region.

Stokes' Law and Terminal Velocity
6-Mark Theory

For a small sphere moving slowly through a viscous fluid, viscous drag increases with velocity. As the sphere accelerates, drag increases until the resultant force becomes zero and terminal velocity is reached.

Typical explanation chain: weight → acceleration → velocity increases → drag increases → resultant decreases → acceleration decreases → terminal velocity.
Force-Extension Graph
Graph Theory

The initial straight-line region represents proportional behaviour. Beyond the elastic limit, permanent deformation may occur.

Unit 2 · Waves & Electricity
WPH12
Wave Properties
Core Theory

A progressive wave transfers energy and information from one location to another without transferring matter overall.

Transverse and Longitudinal Waves
Definitions

In a transverse wave, oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer. In a longitudinal wave, oscillations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer.

Diffraction
6-Mark Theory

Diffraction is the spreading of waves when they pass through a gap or around an obstacle. The effect is most significant when the gap size is comparable to the wavelength.

Interference
Core Theory

Interference occurs when waves overlap. Constructive interference occurs when the waves reinforce each other, while destructive interference occurs when they partially or completely cancel.

Refraction
Core Theory

Refraction occurs when a wave changes speed as it enters another medium. A change in speed causes a change in wavelength; frequency remains constant.

Electric Current
Definitions

Electric current is the rate of flow of charge through a conductor.

Resistance
Core Theory

Resistance describes how strongly a component opposes the flow of electric charge. For an ohmic conductor at constant temperature, current is proportional to potential difference.

Series and Parallel Circuits
Exam Theory

In series, the current is the same through each component. In parallel, the potential difference across each branch is the same.

Unit 3 · Practical Skills I
WPH13
Experimental Variables
Practical Theory

The independent variable is deliberately changed. The dependent variable is measured. Control variables are kept constant to make the investigation fair.

Accuracy, Precision and Resolution
Practical Theory

Accuracy describes closeness to the accepted value. Precision describes how closely repeated measurements agree. Resolution is the smallest change that an instrument can detect.

Uncertainty
Practical Theory

Every measurement has an associated uncertainty. Repeated measurements can be used to estimate random uncertainty, while systematic errors tend to shift all measurements in the same direction.

Best-Fit Lines
Graph Theory

A best-fit line should represent the overall trend of the data rather than simply joining adjacent points.

Unit 4 · Further Mechanics, Fields & Particles
WPH14
Circular Motion
6-Mark Theory

An object moving in a circle continuously changes direction, so it has centripetal acceleration directed towards the centre. The resultant radial force provides this acceleration.

Gravitational Fields
Core Theory

A gravitational field is a region in which a mass experiences a gravitational force. Field strength is force per unit mass.

Electric Fields
Core Theory

An electric field is a region where a charged particle experiences an electric force. Field direction is defined as the direction of force on a positive test charge.

Electric Potential
Definitions

Electric potential at a point is the work done per unit positive charge in bringing a small positive test charge from infinity to that point.

Particle Classification
Particle Physics

Particles can be classified into groups such as leptons, quarks, hadrons and exchange particles. Conservation laws constrain which particle interactions are possible.

Particle Interactions
Exam Theory

Interactions are represented using particle diagrams and must obey conservation of charge, energy, momentum and other relevant quantum numbers.

Unit 5 · Thermodynamics, Radiation, Oscillations & Cosmology
WPH15
Thermal Energy
Core Theory

Temperature is related to the average kinetic energy of particles. Heating transfers energy and can increase temperature or change the state of matter.

Simple Harmonic Motion
6-Mark Theory

Simple harmonic motion occurs when acceleration is directly proportional to displacement from equilibrium and directed towards the equilibrium position.

Radioactive Decay
Core Theory

Radioactive decay is spontaneous and random for individual nuclei. The activity of a sufficiently large sample follows a predictable statistical decay law.

Half-Life
Definition

Half-life is the time required for the number of undecayed nuclei, or the activity, to decrease to half its initial value.

Redshift
Cosmology

Light from a receding astronomical object is observed at a longer wavelength than when emitted. The observed redshift provides evidence about the object's relative motion.

Hubble's Law
Cosmology

For sufficiently distant galaxies, recession speed is approximately proportional to distance. This relationship provides evidence for the expansion of the universe.

Unit 6 · Practical Skills II
WPH16
Planning an Investigation
Practical Theory

A good investigation identifies variables, selects suitable equipment, explains the method, controls relevant variables and considers safety.

Evaluation
6-Mark Practical

Evaluation should identify limitations, distinguish random from systematic errors and propose realistic improvements that directly address the limitation.

Chemistry
Structure · Bonding · Energetics · Rates · Equilibrium · Organic · Transition Metals
Unit 1 · Structure, Bonding & Introduction to Organic Chemistry
WCH11
Atomic Structure
Core Theory

Atoms contain protons and neutrons in the nucleus with electrons occupying shells or orbitals around the nucleus. Isotopes contain the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.

Mass Spectrometry
6-Mark Theory

A mass spectrometer can be used to identify isotopes and determine relative isotopic abundance. Particles are ionised, accelerated, separated according to mass-to-charge ratio and detected.

Ionic Bonding
Definitions

Ionic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions formed following electron transfer.

Covalent Bonding
Definitions

A covalent bond is the electrostatic attraction between a shared pair of electrons and the nuclei of the bonded atoms.

Metallic Bonding
Definitions

Metallic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and delocalised electrons.

Electronegativity
Exam Theory

Electronegativity is the ability of an atom to attract the bonding pair of electrons in a covalent bond.

Organic Functional Groups
Organic Theory

A functional group determines many of the characteristic chemical reactions of an organic compound.

Structural Isomerism
Definitions

Structural isomers have the same molecular formula but different structural arrangements of atoms.

Unit 2 · Energetics, Group Chemistry, Halogenoalkanes & Alcohols
WCH12
Enthalpy Changes
Core Theory

Enthalpy change measures the heat energy transferred during a reaction under constant pressure.

Hess's Law
Long Response

The enthalpy change for a reaction is independent of the route taken between reactants and products because enthalpy is a state function.

Bond Enthalpy
Exam Theory

Average bond enthalpy is the average energy required to break one mole of a particular bond in gaseous molecules.

Group 2 Trends
Inorganic Theory

Down Group 2, atomic radius increases and the outer electrons are further from the nucleus. This affects ionisation energy and chemical reactivity.

Halogenoalkanes
Organic Theory

Halogenoalkanes undergo reactions including nucleophilic substitution. Their reactivity is influenced by the strength of the carbon-halogen bond.

Alcohols
Organic Theory

Alcohols can undergo oxidation, dehydration and reactions associated with the hydroxyl functional group.

Unit 3 · Practical Skills I
WCH13
Titration
Practical Theory

A titration determines the concentration of an unknown solution by reacting it with a solution of known concentration.

Uncertainty in Measurements
Practical Theory

Measured quantities contain uncertainty. The effect of uncertainty should be considered when reporting calculated results.

Qualitative Analysis
Practical Theory

Chemical tests can identify ions and functional groups through characteristic observations such as colour changes, precipitates or gas formation.

Unit 4 · Rates, Equilibrium & Further Organic Chemistry
WCH14
Collision Theory
6-Mark Theory

For a reaction to occur, particles must collide with sufficient energy and suitable orientation. Increasing temperature increases the proportion of particles with energy above the activation energy.

Catalysts
Core Theory

A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. It does not change the equilibrium position.

Dynamic Equilibrium
6-Mark Theory

At dynamic equilibrium, the forward and reverse reactions continue at equal rates. Concentrations remain constant although particles continue to react.

Le Chatelier's Principle
Long Response

When a change is imposed on a system at equilibrium, the system responds in the direction that tends to oppose the change.

Acids and Bases
Core Theory

An acid donates protons while a base accepts protons. Strong acids and bases ionise or dissociate extensively, whereas weak acids and bases establish equilibria.

Organic Reaction Mechanisms
Organic Theory

Reaction mechanisms show how bonds break and form using curly arrows to represent electron movement.

Unit 5 · Transition Metals & Organic Nitrogen Chemistry
WCH15
Transition Metals
Inorganic Theory

Transition metals form ions with partially filled d orbitals and commonly exhibit variable oxidation states, coloured compounds and complex formation.

Complex Ions
Core Theory

A complex ion consists of a central metal ion surrounded by ligands that donate lone pairs to form coordinate bonds.

Ligands
Definitions

A ligand is an ion or molecule that donates a lone pair of electrons to a central metal ion.

Amines
Organic Theory

Amines are derivatives of ammonia in which one or more hydrogen atoms have been replaced by organic groups.

Amino Acids
Organic Theory

Amino acids contain both amine and carboxylic acid functional groups and can behave as both acids and bases.

Unit 6 · Practical Skills II
WCH16
Experimental Design
Practical Theory

A good chemical investigation identifies variables, controls conditions, selects appropriate apparatus and produces repeatable measurements.

Evaluation of Results
6-Mark Practical

An evaluation should identify specific limitations and explain how each limitation affects the result. Improvements should directly address the identified limitation.

Biology
Molecules · Cells · Transport · Energy · Environment · Genetics · Gene Technology
Unit 1 · Molecules, Diet, Transport & Health
WBI11
Carbohydrates
Core Biology

Carbohydrates include monosaccharides, disaccharides and polysaccharides. Monosaccharides join through condensation reactions to form larger carbohydrates.

Proteins
Core Biology

Proteins are polymers of amino acids. The sequence of amino acids determines the primary structure and influences higher levels of protein structure.

Lipids
Core Biology

Triglycerides contain glycerol and three fatty acids. Phospholipids have hydrophilic phosphate-containing heads and hydrophobic fatty-acid tails.

Enzymes
6-Mark Theory

Enzymes are biological catalysts. Their active site has a specific shape complementary to the substrate. Temperature, pH and substrate concentration can affect enzyme activity.

Cell Membranes
6-Mark Theory

Cell membranes contain phospholipids and proteins arranged according to the fluid mosaic model. Membrane proteins perform functions including transport, recognition and reception.

Gas Exchange
Long Response

Efficient gas exchange surfaces have a large surface area, short diffusion distance and suitable concentration gradients. Ventilation and blood flow help maintain concentration gradients.

Transport in Plants
Core Biology

Xylem transports water and mineral ions. Phloem transports assimilates such as sucrose between sources and sinks.

Unit 2 · Cells, Development, Biodiversity & Conservation
WBI12
Cell Ultrastructure
Core Biology

Eukaryotic cells contain membrane-bound organelles with specialised functions. Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus and have a simpler internal organisation.

Mitosis
6-Mark Theory

Mitosis produces genetically identical daughter cells. It is important for growth, repair and asexual reproduction.

Meiosis
6-Mark Theory

Meiosis produces genetically different haploid cells. Variation arises through independent assortment and crossing over.

DNA Replication
Long Response

DNA replication is semi-conservative. Each original strand acts as a template for a new complementary strand.

Inheritance
Genetics

Alleles are alternative forms of a gene. Genotype describes the alleles present while phenotype describes the observable characteristics.

Natural Selection
6-Mark Theory

Individuals within a population vary. Individuals with advantageous heritable characteristics are more likely to survive and reproduce, increasing the frequency of those alleles over generations.

Biodiversity and Conservation
Extended Theory

Biodiversity refers to variation within and between species and ecosystems. Conservation aims to maintain biodiversity and prevent species loss.

Unit 3 · Practical Skills in Biology I
WBI13
Microscopy
Practical Theory

Microscopes allow structures smaller than the unaided eye can resolve to be observed. Magnification and resolution are different properties.

Sampling
Practical Theory

Random sampling reduces selection bias. Systematic and stratified methods may be used where the population has known structure.

Variables and Controls
Practical Theory

A biological investigation should clearly identify the independent and dependent variables and control relevant environmental factors.

Validity and Reliability
Evaluation

A valid investigation measures what it is intended to measure. Reliability increases when repeated measurements produce consistent results.

Unit 4 · Energy, Environment, Microbiology & Immunity
WBI14
Photosynthesis
6-Mark Theory

Light energy is converted into chemical energy during photosynthesis. Light-dependent reactions generate ATP and reduced NADP, which support carbon fixation reactions.

Respiration
6-Mark Theory

Aerobic respiration transfers energy from glucose to ATP. Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm while later stages occur in mitochondria.

Energy Transfer in Ecosystems
Long Response

Energy enters many ecosystems through photosynthesis and passes between trophic levels. Energy is lost through respiration and other processes at each transfer.

Nutrient Cycles
Core Biology

Elements such as carbon and nitrogen cycle between organisms and the physical environment through biological and chemical processes.

Microorganisms
Core Biology

Microorganisms include bacteria and fungi. They can be beneficial in biotechnology and decomposition but can also cause disease.

Immune Response
6-Mark Theory

The immune system recognises foreign antigens and produces specific responses involving lymphocytes, antibodies and memory cells.

Vaccination
Long Response

Vaccination exposes the immune system to an antigen or antigenic material so that memory cells are produced. A later exposure can therefore trigger a faster secondary immune response.

Unit 5 · Respiration, Internal Environment, Coordination & Gene Technology
WBI15
Homeostasis
6-Mark Theory

Homeostasis maintains a relatively stable internal environment despite external or internal changes. Negative feedback returns conditions towards a set point.

Nervous Coordination
Core Biology

The nervous system coordinates rapid responses using electrical impulses transmitted along neurones.

Synapses
6-Mark Theory

At a chemical synapse, neurotransmitter is released from the presynaptic neurone, diffuses across the synaptic cleft and binds to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane.

Hormonal Coordination
Core Biology

Hormones are chemical messengers transported in the blood. They bind to specific receptors on target cells.

Gene Expression
Genetics

Gene expression involves transcription of DNA into RNA and translation of the genetic information into a polypeptide.

Gene Technology
6-Mark Theory

Gene technology involves techniques used to isolate, amplify, modify or transfer genetic material. Applications include medicine, agriculture and research.

Hardy-Weinberg Principle
Genetics

The Hardy-Weinberg principle describes allele and genotype frequencies in an ideal population where evolutionary forces such as selection, mutation and migration are absent.

Unit 6 · Practical Skills in Biology II
WBI16
Experimental Design
Practical Theory

A strong investigation identifies the hypothesis, variables, controls, method, repeats, appropriate equipment and safety considerations.

Data Presentation
Practical Theory

Tables should include appropriate headings and units. Graphs should use suitable scales, labelled axes and appropriate plotting conventions.

Statistical Testing
Data Analysis

Statistical tests are used to determine whether observed differences or relationships are likely to have arisen by chance.

Evaluation
6-Mark Practical

An effective evaluation identifies specific limitations, explains their effect on the results and proposes practical improvements.

Avoid vague statements such as “human error”. State exactly what went wrong, how it affected the result and how the method could be improved.
