LESSON 01

Vectors and Scalars

Mechanics contains quantities that describe both the size of something and the direction in which it acts. These are vector quantities.

Scalar

A scalar has magnitude only.

  • Mass
  • Time
  • Distance
  • Speed
  • Energy

Vector

A vector has both magnitude and direction.

  • Displacement
  • Velocity
  • Acceleration
  • Force
Vector quantity

A physical quantity described by both a magnitude and a direction.

Important distinction

Distance and displacement are not the same. Distance is the total length travelled and is scalar. Displacement describes the directed change in position and is vector.

LESSON 02

Representing Vectors

A vector can be represented geometrically by a directed line segment. The arrowhead indicates direction and the length of the line represents its magnitude.

Magnitude

The size or length of the vector.

Direction

The orientation in which the vector acts.

Equal vectors

Two vectors are equal if they have the same magnitude and the same direction, regardless of where they are drawn.

A vector may therefore be translated parallel to itself without changing the vector.

Exam tip

Do not decide whether two vectors are equal merely because they are drawn in different positions. Compare their magnitude and direction.

LESSON 03

Position Vectors

A position vector describes the position of a point relative to a chosen origin.

Position vector

The vector from the origin O to a point P is called the position vector of P.

Using Cartesian unit vectors, a point with coordinates (x, y) has position vector x i + y j.

OP = xi + yj

Here:

  • i is the unit vector in the positive x-direction.
  • j is the unit vector in the positive y-direction.
  • x and y are the horizontal and vertical components.

WORKED EXAMPLE 01 • Position Vector

Point A has coordinates (4, −3). Find the position vector OA.

The x-coordinate is 4.

The y-coordinate is −3.

OA = 4i − 3j

LESSON 04

Unit Vectors

A unit vector is a vector with magnitude exactly 1.

|i| = 1      |j| = 1

The standard Cartesian unit vectors are:

i

Unit vector in the positive x-direction.

j

Unit vector in the positive y-direction.

Therefore a vector such as 3i + 4j has horizontal component 3 and vertical component 4.

a = xi + yj

Its magnitude is found using Pythagoras.

|a| = √(x² + y²)
Notation warning

The coefficient of i or j is a component, not automatically the magnitude of the entire vector.

LESSON 05

Magnitude and Direction

For a vector a = xi + yj, the magnitude is:

|a| = √(x² + y²)

The direction can be described using an angle measured from a chosen axis.

tan θ = y / x

The formula above must be interpreted carefully because the signs of x and y determine the quadrant.

WORKED EXAMPLE 02 • Magnitude

Find the magnitude of a = 6i + 8j.

|a| = √(6² + 8²)

|a| = √(36 + 64)

|a| = √100

|a| = 10

WORKED EXAMPLE 03 • Direction

A vector has components 5i + 12j. Find the angle it makes with the positive x-axis.

tan θ = 12 / 5

θ = tan⁻¹(12/5)

θ ≈ 67.4°

Quadrant trap

If a component is negative, the vector may lie in the second, third or fourth quadrant. Do not simply quote the calculator's inverse-tangent result without checking the direction.

LESSON 06

Vector Addition, Subtraction and Multiplication

Vectors written in component form can be manipulated component by component.

(a i + b j) + (c i + d j) = (a+c)i + (b+d)j
(a i + b j) − (c i + d j) = (a−c)i + (b−d)j
k(a i + b j) = ka i + kb j

WORKED EXAMPLE 04 • Addition

Let a = 3i + 5j and b = 2i − 7j. Find a + b.

a + b = (3 + 2)i + (5 − 7)j

a + b = 5i − 2j

WORKED EXAMPLE 05 • Scalar Multiplication

Find −3(2i − 4j).

Multiply every component by −3.

−3(2i − 4j) = −6i + 12j

Answer: −6i + 12j

LESSON 07

Displacement Vectors

If a particle moves from point A to point B, its displacement is the vector from A to B.

AB = OB − OA

This formula is one of the most important vector relationships in mechanics.

WORKED EXAMPLE 06 • Displacement

A particle moves from A(2, 3) to B(8, 7). Find its displacement.

OA = 2i + 3j

OB = 8i + 7j

AB = OB − OA

AB = (8 − 2)i + (7 − 3)j

AB = 6i + 4j

Remember the order

Displacement from A to B is B minus A. Reversing the order gives the displacement from B to A.

LESSON 08

Velocity as a Vector

Velocity describes the rate of change of displacement and has both magnitude and direction.

velocity = displacement / time

In vector form, if a particle has displacement vector s over a time interval t, its average velocity is:

v = s / t

In component form:

v = (x/t)i + (y/t)j

WORKED EXAMPLE 07 • Average Velocity

A particle moves through displacement 12i + 5j metres in 4 seconds. Find its average velocity.

v = (12i + 5j)/4

v = 3i + 1.25j m s⁻¹

Velocity versus speed

Speed is the magnitude of velocity. Velocity itself includes direction.

LESSON 09

Acceleration as a Vector

Acceleration describes the rate of change of velocity.

a = change in velocity / time

If a particle changes velocity from u to v over time t:

a = (v − u) / t

WORKED EXAMPLE 08 • Acceleration Vector

A particle has initial velocity 2i + 3j m s⁻¹ and final velocity 10i + 7j m s⁻¹ after 4 seconds. Find its acceleration.

Change in velocity:

v − u = (10i + 7j) − (2i + 3j)

= 8i + 4j

Therefore:

a = (8i + 4j)/4

a = 2i + j m s⁻²

Core idea

A particle can accelerate even if its speed is unchanged, because a change in direction is also a change in velocity.

LESSON 10

Relative Position and Displacement

Vector subtraction can be used to determine the position of one particle relative to another.

If particles A and B have position vectors a and b respectively, then:

AB = b − a

This is simply the position of B minus the position of A.

WORKED EXAMPLE 09 • Relative Position

At a particular instant, A has position vector 4i + 2j and B has position vector 9i − 4j. Find AB.

AB = OB − OA

AB = (9i − 4j) − (4i + 2j)

AB = 5i − 6j

AB = 5i − 6j

Direction matters

AB and BA have the same magnitude but opposite directions.

LESSON 11

Vector Equations of Motion

Mechanics equations can be written using vectors by treating each component consistently.

v = u + at

This can be applied directly to vector quantities when the acceleration is constant.

v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as

When using vector equations, each term must have compatible vector units and the vector directions must be handled correctly.

WORKED EXAMPLE 10 • Constant Acceleration

A particle has initial velocity 3i + 2j m s⁻¹ and constant acceleration i − 2j m s⁻². Find its velocity after 5 seconds.

v = u + at

v = (3i + 2j) + (i − 2j)(5)

v = 3i + 2j + 5i − 10j

v = 8i − 8j m s⁻¹

Component method

If you find a vector equation difficult, separate it into its i-component and j-component and solve each independently.

LESSON 12

Exam Method: A Reliable Vector Workflow

Most vector errors in mechanics are not difficult mathematics; they are caused by incorrect interpretation or sign errors.

  1. Identify the points and directions.
  2. Write position vectors clearly.
  3. Use final position minus initial position for displacement.
  4. Keep the i and j components separate.
  5. Check signs carefully.
  6. Use Pythagoras for magnitude.
  7. Use trigonometry for direction.
  8. Include correct units where appropriate.
Displacement = final position − initial position
Velocity = displacement ÷ time
Acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time

LESSON 13

Advanced Worked Examples

EXAMPLE 11 • Distance Between Two Points

A has position vector 2i − 5j and B has position vector 8i + 3j. Find the distance AB.

AB = (8 − 2)i + (3 − (−5))j

AB = 6i + 8j

|AB| = √(6² + 8²)

|AB| = 10

EXAMPLE 12 • Finding an Unknown Component

A vector has magnitude 13 and is 5i + kj. Find the possible values of k.

√(5² + k²) = 13

25 + k² = 169

k² = 144

k = ±12

EXAMPLE 13 • Equal Vectors

If a = (2p + 1)i + 7j and b = 9i + 7j are equal, find p.

Equal vectors have equal corresponding components.

2p + 1 = 9

2p = 8

p = 4

EXAMPLE 14 • Finding Time from Displacement

A particle travels with constant velocity 4i + 3j m s⁻¹. Find its displacement after 6 seconds.

s = vt

s = (4i + 3j)(6)

s = 24i + 18j m

EXAMPLE 15 • Position After a Given Time

A particle starts at position 2i − j metres. It moves with constant velocity 3i + 4j m s⁻¹. Find its position after 5 seconds.

Final position = initial position + displacement.

Displacement = vt

= (3i + 4j)(5)

= 15i + 20j

Final position:

(2i − j) + (15i + 20j)

17i + 19j m

EXAMPLE 16 • Finding Acceleration

A particle's velocity changes from 7i − 2j to 3i + 10j in 4 seconds. Find the acceleration.

Δv = (3i + 10j) − (7i − 2j)

Δv = −4i + 12j

a = Δv / 4

a = −i + 3j m s⁻²

LESSON 14

Common Exam Traps

Trap 01 — Reversing displacement

AB = B − A, not A − B.

Trap 02 — Confusing distance with displacement

Distance is scalar. Displacement is vector.

Trap 03 — Forgetting negative components

A negative i or j component represents direction opposite to the positive axis.

Trap 04 — Incorrect magnitude

For xi + yj, magnitude is √(x² + y²), not x + y.

Trap 05 — Wrong direction angle

Always consider the signs of both components before reporting a direction.

Trap 06 — Mixing units

Position/displacement should normally be in metres, velocity in m s⁻¹ and acceleration in m s⁻².

Trap 07 — Treating velocity as speed

The magnitude of velocity gives speed, but velocity itself includes direction.

Golden rule

Write the vector relationship first. Substitute numbers second. This dramatically reduces sign and direction errors.

Vector Formula Sheet

Core relationships to memorise

Position → displacement → velocity → acceleration. Each step involves comparing the appropriate vector quantity over time.
