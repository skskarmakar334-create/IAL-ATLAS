01 / Start Here

The Big Ideas of Economics

Economics studies how people, businesses and governments make choices when resources are limited.

S

Scarcity

Resources are limited, but people's wants are unlimited. Scarcity means we cannot have everything we want.

Example: You have ৳500 but want to buy books, food and a game. You cannot necessarily afford everything.
C

Choice

Because resources are limited, individuals and societies must decide how to use them.

Example: A government decides how much money to spend on education, healthcare and transport.
OC

Opportunity Cost

The value of the next best alternative given up when making a choice.

Example: If you study instead of watching a film, the film is your opportunity cost if it was your next best option.

Remember the three basic economic questions

  • What to produce? Which goods and services should be made?
  • How to produce? Which resources and methods should be used?
  • For whom to produce? Who will receive the goods and services?
02 / Learning Roadmap

Explore Economics Topics

Search for a topic or select a category. Each card introduces the main idea without replacing your full class notes.

TOPIC 01

Basic Economic Problem

Scarcity, choice, opportunity cost and resource allocation.

  • Land, labour, capital and enterprise
  • Needs versus wants
  • Production possibility frontiers (PPFs)
Think about it: What must a country give up when it uses more resources to produce healthcare?
TOPIC 02

Economic Systems

How societies organise production and distribute resources.

  • Market economy
  • Planned economy
  • Mixed economy
Example: In a mixed economy, private businesses operate alongside government services.
TOPIC 03

Demand and Supply

How buyers and sellers influence market prices and quantities.

  • Demand and supply curves
  • Movements along curves
  • Shifts in curves
Example: If demand rises while supply stays the same, equilibrium price tends to rise.
TOPIC 04

Elasticity

Measures how responsive one economic variable is to another.

  • Price elasticity of demand (PED)
  • Income elasticity of demand (YED)
  • Cross elasticity (XED) and PES
PED = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price
Example: If a small price rise causes a large fall in demand, demand is relatively elastic.
TOPIC 05

Market Structures

How the number and size of firms affect competition.

  • Competitive markets
  • Monopoly
  • Barriers to entry
Example: A firm with strong market power may have greater influence over price.
TOPIC 06

Market Failure

When markets fail to allocate resources efficiently for society.

  • Externalities
  • Public goods
  • Information gaps and market power
Example: Pollution from a factory can impose costs on people who are not involved in the transaction.
TOPIC 07

Macroeconomic Objectives

The main goals governments may try to achieve in an economy.

  • Economic growth
  • Low and stable inflation
  • Low unemployment
  • Balance of payments stability
Think about it: Can policies that reduce inflation also slow economic growth?
TOPIC 08

Aggregate Demand and Supply

Explains total spending and total output in an economy.

  • Aggregate demand (AD)
  • Short-run aggregate supply (SRAS)
  • Long-run aggregate supply (LRAS)
AD = C + I + G + (X − M)
Example: Higher consumer confidence may increase consumption and aggregate demand.
TOPIC 09

GDP and Economic Growth

Understand the value of national output and changes over time.

  • Gross Domestic Product (GDP)
  • Real versus nominal GDP
  • Benefits and costs of growth
Remember: Real GDP adjusts for changes in the general price level.
TOPIC 10

Inflation and Unemployment

Two important indicators of economic performance.

  • Inflation and purchasing power
  • Frictional unemployment
  • Structural and cyclical unemployment
Example: If prices rise faster than income, households can afford fewer goods and services.
TOPIC 11

Economic Policies

How governments and central banks influence economic activity.

  • Fiscal policy: taxation and government spending
  • Monetary policy: interest rates and monetary tools
  • Supply-side policies
Example: Lower interest rates may encourage borrowing and spending, other things equal.
TOPIC 12

International Economics

How countries trade and interact with the world economy.

  • Specialisation and comparative advantage
  • Exchange rates
  • Tariffs, quotas and trade
  • Current account
Example: A tariff is a tax on imported goods and may increase their domestic price.

03 / Learn by Example

Economics in Everyday Life

Connect basic concepts with decisions you already understand.

01

A Price Increase

A shop increases the price of a product. Some customers buy less.

Concept: The law of demand suggests that, other things equal, a higher price leads to a lower quantity demanded.
02

Limited Resources

A student has two hours and must choose between studying and working.

Concept: Scarcity forces a choice, and the next best alternative forgone is the opportunity cost.
03

Rising Prices

The average price of many goods and services increases over time.

Concept: This is inflation. It can reduce the purchasing power of money if income does not keep pace.
