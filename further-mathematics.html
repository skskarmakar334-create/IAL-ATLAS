01 — Complex Numbers

A complex number has a real part and an imaginary part. The imaginary unit \(i\) satisfies \(i^2=-1\).

z = a + bi
Re(z) = a
Im(z) = b
i² = −1

Example: if \(z=3+4i\), its real part is 3 and its imaginary part is 4.

|z| = √(a² + b²)

For \(z=3+4i\), the modulus is \(\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\).

02 — Complex Conjugates

The conjugate of a complex number is formed by changing the sign of its imaginary part.

z = a + bi
z̄ = a − bi
z z̄ = a² + b²

Example: the conjugate of \(2+3i\) is \(2-3i\).

Multiplying a complex number by its conjugate produces a real number.

03 — Matrices

A matrix is a rectangular arrangement of numbers. Addition requires matrices of the same dimensions.

A = [a b; c d]
det(A) = ad − bc

For a 2 × 2 matrix, the determinant is the top-left entry times the bottom-right entry minus the other diagonal product.

A⁻¹ = 1/(ad − bc) [d −b; −c a]

The inverse exists only when the determinant is non-zero.

04 — Vectors

A vector has magnitude and direction. In two dimensions, its components can be written as \((a,b)\).

|(a,b)| = √(a² + b²)

Example: the magnitude of \((3,4)\) is 5.

(a,b) · (c,d) = ac + bd

If the scalar product is zero, two non-zero vectors are perpendicular.

05 — Differentiation

Differentiation measures the instantaneous rate of change of a function.

d/dx (xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹

Example:

y = x³
dy/dx = 3x²

At a stationary point, the first derivative is zero, though the point may be a maximum, minimum or stationary point of inflection.

06 — Integration

Integration reverses differentiation for many functions and can calculate accumulated quantities.

∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C, n ≠ −1

Example:

∫3x² dx = x³ + C

The constant \(C\) appears in indefinite integration because a constant disappears when differentiated.

07 — Arithmetic Progressions

An arithmetic progression has a constant difference between consecutive terms.

uₙ = a + (n − 1)d
Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n − 1)d]

Here \(a\) is the first term and \(d\) is the common difference.

08 — Geometric Progressions

A geometric progression has a constant ratio between consecutive terms.

uₙ = arⁿ⁻¹
Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r), r ≠ 1

For an infinite geometric series, the sum exists when \(|r|<1\):

S∞ = a/(1 − r)

09 — Binomial Expansion

For a non-negative integer \(n\), the binomial theorem expands a power of a sum.

(a + b)ⁿ = Σ ⁿCᵣ aⁿ⁻ʳbʳ

Example:

(x + 2)² = x² + 4x + 4

The coefficients come from binomial coefficients, which can be calculated using factorials.

ⁿCᵣ = n! / [r!(n − r)!]

10 — Differential Equations

A differential equation relates a function to one or more of its derivatives.

dy/dx = ky

One general solution is:

y = Aeᵏˣ

The constant \(A\) is determined using an initial condition when one is given.

11 — Mathematical Proof

A proof is a logically complete argument showing that a statement follows from accepted facts or assumptions.

Direct proof: begin with known information and logically derive the result.

Contradiction: assume the statement is false and show that this leads to a contradiction.

A few examples supporting a statement do not, by themselves, prove a universal mathematical claim.

12 — Numerical Methods

Numerical methods can approximate solutions when exact algebraic solutions are difficult to obtain.

xₙ₊₁ = g(xₙ)

In fixed-point iteration, repeatedly apply the function \(g\) to the previous approximation.

Convergence depends on the function and starting value. Always consider the required accuracy and check whether the method converges.
