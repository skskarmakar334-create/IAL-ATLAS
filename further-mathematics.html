01 — Complex Numbers

A complex number contains a real part and an imaginary part.

z = a + bi
i² = −1
Re(z) = a, Im(z) = b
Example: If z = 3 + 4i, the real part is 3 and the imaginary part is 4.

The modulus tells us the distance of the complex number from the origin.

|z| = √(a² + b²)
For 3 + 4i: |z| = √(9 + 16) = 5.

02 — Complex Conjugates

The conjugate is obtained by changing the sign of the imaginary part.

z = a + bi
z̄ = a − bi
z z̄ = a² + b²
Example: The conjugate of 2 + 3i is 2 − 3i.

Multiplying a complex number by its conjugate gives a real number.

03 — Matrices

A matrix is a rectangular arrangement of numbers. You can add matrices of the same dimensions by adding corresponding entries.

A = [a b; c d]
det(A) = ad − bc
Example: For A = [2 1; 3 4], det(A) = 2 × 4 − 1 × 3 = 5.

A 2 × 2 matrix has an inverse only when its determinant is not zero.

04 — Vectors

A vector has magnitude and direction. In two dimensions, it can be written as (a, b).

|(a, b)| = √(a² + b²)
Example: The magnitude of (3, 4) is √(9 + 16) = 5.
(a, b) · (c, d) = ac + bd

For two non-zero vectors, a scalar product of zero means they are perpendicular.

05 — Differentiation

Differentiation measures the rate at which a function changes. It can help find gradients and stationary points.

d/dx (xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹
Example: If y = x³, then dy/dx = 3x².

At a stationary point, the first derivative is zero. Further checking may be needed to identify the type of point.

06 — Integration

Integration reverses differentiation for many functions and can calculate areas or accumulated quantities.

∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1) + C, n ≠ −1
Example: ∫3x² dx = x³ + C.

The constant C is included in indefinite integration because the derivative of any constant is zero.

07 — Arithmetic Progressions

An arithmetic progression has a constant difference between consecutive terms.

nth term: uₙ = a + (n − 1)d
Sum: Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n − 1)d]
Example: In 3, 7, 11, 15, ... the common difference is 4.

Here a is the first term and d is the common difference.

08 — Geometric Progressions

A geometric progression has a constant ratio between consecutive terms.

nth term: uₙ = arⁿ⁻¹
Sum: Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r), r ≠ 1
Example: In 2, 6, 18, 54, ... the common ratio is 3.

An infinite geometric series has a finite sum when |r| < 1.

S∞ = a/(1 − r)

09 — Binomial Expansion

The binomial theorem helps expand powers of a sum.

(a + b)ⁿ = Σ ⁿCᵣ aⁿ⁻ʳbʳ
Example: (x + 2)² = x² + 4x + 4.

The binomial coefficient can be calculated using factorials.

ⁿCᵣ = n! / [r!(n − r)!]

10 — Differential Equations

A differential equation connects a function with one or more of its derivatives.

dy/dx = ky

For constant k, a general solution is:

y = Aeᵏˣ
Key idea: A given initial condition can help determine the constant A.

11 — Mathematical Proof

A proof is a logical argument that demonstrates why a mathematical statement follows from accepted facts or assumptions.

  • Direct proof: Start with known information and derive the result.
  • Contradiction: Assume the opposite and show that it leads to a contradiction.
  • Counterexample: One valid counterexample can disprove a universal claim.
Checking a few examples does not, by itself, prove that a statement is always true.

12 — Numerical Methods

Numerical methods approximate solutions when an exact solution is difficult to obtain.

xₙ₊₁ = g(xₙ)
Example: Start with an estimate, apply the iteration formula, and repeat to obtain a sequence of approximations.

Check whether the method converges and whether the final answer meets the required accuracy.

How to study Further Mathematics

  1. Understand what each symbol in a formula represents.
  2. Follow a worked example step by step.
  3. Try a similar question without looking at the answer.
  4. Check your working and identify where a mistake occurred.
  5. Use your official specification and teacher's materials for complete syllabus coverage.
