WPH11 · Unit 1

Mechanics

First Equation of Motion

v = u + at
Relates initial velocity, final velocity, acceleration and time for uniformly accelerated motion.
u = initial velocity · v = final velocity · a = acceleration · t = time

Second Equation of Motion

s = ut + ½at²
Gives displacement for motion with constant acceleration.
s = displacement · u = initial velocity · a = acceleration · t = time

Third Equation of Motion

v² = u² + 2as
Useful when time is not given in a uniformly accelerated motion problem.

Displacement Equation

s = ½(u + v)t
Displacement for uniformly accelerated motion.

Newton's Second Law

ΣF = ma
The resultant force on an object equals its mass multiplied by its acceleration.

Weight

W = mg
Weight is the gravitational force acting on a mass.
W = weight · m = mass · g = gravitational field strength

Gravitational Field Strength

g = F / m
Gravitational force per unit mass.

Momentum

p = mv
Momentum is the product of mass and velocity.

Moment of a Force

Moment = Fx
The turning effect of a force about an axis.
F = force · x = perpendicular distance from axis

Work Done

ΔW = FΔs
Work done when a force acts through a displacement in the direction of the force.

Kinetic Energy

Eₖ = ½mv²
Energy associated with the motion of an object.

Gravitational Potential Energy

ΔEgrav = mgΔh
Change in gravitational potential energy near the Earth's surface.

Power from Energy

P = E / t
Power is the rate of energy transfer.

Power from Work

P = W / t
Power is the rate at which work is done.

Efficiency from Energy

η = useful energy output / total energy input
Efficiency compares useful energy output with total energy input.

Efficiency from Power

η = useful power output / total power input
Efficiency can also be calculated using useful and total power.
Materials

Density

ρ = m / V
Density is mass per unit volume.
ρ = density · m = mass · V = volume

Stokes' Law

F = 6πηrv
Viscous drag on a small sphere moving slowly through a fluid.
F = drag force · η = viscosity · r = radius · v = speed

Hooke's Law

ΔF = kΔx
Within the limit of proportionality, extension is proportional to applied force.
k = stiffness · Δx = extension

Tensile / Compressive Stress

Stress = F / A
Force per unit cross-sectional area.
F = force · A = cross-sectional area

Strain

Strain = ΔL / L
Change in length divided by original length.
ΔL = change in length · L = original length

Young Modulus

E = stress / strain
Ratio of tensile or compressive stress to strain.

Elastic Strain Energy

Eel = ½FΔx
Elastic strain energy stored in a material when the force-extension relationship is linear.

Elastic Energy from Graph

Eel = area under force-extension graph
The area under a force-extension graph represents elastic strain energy. For non-linear graphs, the area may need to be estimated.
